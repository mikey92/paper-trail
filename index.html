Paper Trail

A field card for this week's walk.

Tell it where and when. It finds what people actually reported there in past years at this time of year, asks Gemma, running inside this tab, for one line on how to recognise each, and gives you a single page to print. Then the phone goes back in your pocket.

How it works

  1. What's there. iNaturalist's research-grade observations near the place, in the same month of every year, grouped so the card isn't all birds.
  2. What it looks like. The description section of each species' Wikipedia article, cut down to the sentences about colours, shapes, sounds and smells.
  3. One line each. Gemma 3 turns those sentences into a tip, in a Web Worker on your own GPU (or CPU). Every word of the tip has to appear in the sentences, and every colour has to sit next to the same part; a tip that fails is retried once with the offending words named, then replaced by a quote.
  4. Paper. Weather and sunset from Open-Meteo, a box to sketch in, and the sources at the bottom.

The page has no server of its own and keeps nothing. A location from your device is rounded to about a kilometre before it is sent to iNaturalist and Open-Meteo; the model never sees it. Code and evaluation: github.com/mikey92/paper-trail (MIT).
